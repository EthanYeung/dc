# Distribution Playbook

## Objective

Build durable, low-cost access to a clearly defined valuable audience while continuously testing whether that audience can support future economic value.

Distribution is not synonymous with traffic.

## 1. Define the audience

Specify:
- target customer;
- painful recurring job;
- qualification criteria;
- economic value of the problem;
- where the user already searches, works or asks for help.

Do not optimize acquisition until the audience definition is concrete.

## 2. Find recurring demand

Look for repeated tasks, queries, deadlines, calculations, documents, checks, monitoring needs, comparisons or workflows.

Prefer demand that creates a reason to return.

## 3. Choose the audience wedge

Prefer the smallest useful artifact that attracts or serves the target user:
- calculator;
- checker;
- generator;
- searchable database;
- benchmark;
- checklist;
- monitoring alert;
- workflow utility;
- template with personalized output;
- lightweight integration.

Pure content may be used when appropriate, but utility is preferred when it can generate stronger intent, repeat use or reusable data.

## 4. Acquire qualified users

Start with the cheapest credible channel.

Measure:
- total reach;
- qualified-user rate;
- cost per qualified user;
- engagement;
- repeat use.

Do not celebrate raw traffic without qualification.

## 5. Convert rented reach into durable relationships

Where lawful and appropriate, give users a reason to:
- create an account;
- save work;
- subscribe to useful updates;
- return to the tool;
- connect a workflow;
- explicitly opt in to a direct relationship.

Do not dark-pattern, coerce, or collect unnecessary personal data.

## 6. Measure commercial intent

Examples:
- repeated high-value use;
- request for a missing capability;
- qualified inquiry;
- pricing-page interaction;
- demo request;
- waitlist;
- request to save/export/share business-critical work;
- request for managed service;
- payment or deposit.

Commercial signals should become structured evidence.

## 7. Escalate deliberately

Move toward paid validation when:
- the audience can be reached reliably;
- repeated behavior confirms the problem;
- commercial intent is visible;
- the proposed paid offer is a natural extension of the existing utility or workflow.

Do not wait for large traffic if strong commercial signals appear early.

## 8. Compound the asset

Use what is learned to improve:
- utility;
- data;
- search/discovery surfaces;
- onboarding;
- retention;
- segmentation;
- future products.

The desired flywheel is:

Distribution
-> Behavior/Data
-> Better Understanding
-> Product/Service
-> Revenue
-> Better Distribution

## Stop conditions

Stop or redesign a distribution initiative when:
- qualified-user rate remains poor after a bounded test;
- repeat use is absent where repeat use is expected;
- acquisition depends on uneconomic paid traffic;
- platform dependency creates unacceptable fragility;
- commercial-intent signals remain absent;
- legal, privacy or platform risk exceeds expected value.
